Under this heading I include quantum computing, quantum information theory and classical information theory.  Also cybernetics (including neural networks, control and feedback systems), cryptography, cryptocurrency, machine learning, LLMs, A.I., and neuroscience connections.

These are matters about which I would like to know a lot more.  My interest is as someone with a mathematics background rather than a physics or computer science background.

## Quantum Theory

From the perspective of quantum computation and quantum information theory, one can essentially restrict to finite dimensional state spaces. All the quantum weirdness (superposition, entanglement, teleportation, no cloning,  ... ) is still present. But one separates away the technical issues associated with operators defined on infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces.

The following emphasise the finite dimensional setting. They are all well written. What to read depends on one's background.  And what is good for a first reading or study is probably not the same as for the second or third time around.  

I have only looked briefly at the book and other material by Cottrell, but they do appear to be a good place to start.  Potential quantum misconceptions are addressed, but the pace of the mathematics is a little slow if you already have studied a good first course in linear algebra.

The book by Ekert is excellent, but is a little more from the physics rather than the mathematics perspective. The "standard" quantum axioms/postulates are not explicitly stated. 

Nielsen and Chuang's book is considered to be the "bible".  Although written in 2000 it is still very relevant.

Preskill's notes are also considered to be classics.  But they are not an entry point to the material.

Scott Aaronson's notes and other material (including his blog) are very good, and often quite entertaining.  

The material by Josza and that by Vedral are both very good, but more succinct and so more suitable for the second time around.
